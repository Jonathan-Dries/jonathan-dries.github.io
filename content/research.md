My research focuses on **Cultural & Institutional Economics**. I am particularly interested in how deep-rooted cultural structures interact with formal institutions and affect economic development.

## Job Market Paper

### **The Cultural Foundations of State Legitimacy: Kinship and Institutional Mismatch**
> **Abstract:** This paper investigates the economic cost of the mismatch between formal legal rules and informal kinship norms. Using a spatial Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) across partitioned ethnic homelands, I show that imposing rigid Civil Law on societies with tight kinship norms reduces local economic activity by **11%** and aggregate GDP by **4%**.
>
> I identify the mechanism as a loss of state legitimacy, validated using survey micro-data and evidence from the 2016 Anglophone Crisis in Cameroon.

## Working Papers

### **Rational Superstition: Environmental Volatility and the Origins of Magical Beliefs**
*(Solo)*
> **Abstract:** A theoretical and empirical exploration of how environmental unpredictability shapes cultural beliefs. The project argues that "superstition" and magical beliefs emerge rationally as an insurance mechanism and coping strategy in high-volatility environments. I test whether these beliefs persist even when formal insurance mechanisms are introduced.

* [**Download PDF**](pdfs/superstition.pdf)

### **Corporations as the State: Concessions, Urbanization, and Long-Run Development**
> **Abstract:** Analyzes the long-run persistence of corporate governance in the Copperbelt. By contrasting areas historically governed by private corporate concessions versus those under colonial state administration, I track divergent outcomes in urbanization and development.

## Selected Work in Progress

### **The Radcliffe-Brown Hypothesis: Joking Relationships and Conflict De-escalation**
* **Status:** Early Empirics
* **Summary:** Testing the anthropological theory that "Joking Relationships" (mandated disrespect between groups) act as a conflict-resolution technology. Using a dyadic matrix approach and ACLED data to test if these norms reduce communal violence.

### **The Mabati Revolution: Corrugated Roofs as Water Technology**
* **Status:** Identification Strategy Developed
* **Summary:** Re-evaluating the 1960s "Mabati Women's Groups" in Kenya. Using a triple-difference strategy (Time × Roof × Rain), I classify steel roofs as water catchment technology to show their impact on female school attendance via reduced labor for water collection.

### **Rooted in Place: Tubers, Trade, and Transformation**
*(Co-authored)*
* **Status:** Ongoing
* **Summary:** Arguing that reliance on roots and tubers as staple crops impedes structural transformation due to high trade costs (bulkiness/perishability), creating multilateral resistance to trade.

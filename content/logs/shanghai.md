Let me compare two airport trains. In Rome, the "Leonardo Express" runs from Termini to Fiumicino. Speed: 60 km/h. Price: fourteen euros. In Shanghai, the Maglev, a magnetic levitation monorail, runs from the airport to the city. Speed: 300 km/h. Price: six euros. I rest my case.

Shanghai is the crown jewel of China's modern development, and it lets you know immediately. The infrastructure isn't just functional; it's a statement. Everything is faster, smoother, more futuristic than it has any right to be. The Maglev hits its top speed within minutes and the landscape outside the window becomes an impressionist blur.

---

The skyline is iconic for good reason. Standing on the Bund, the historical waterfront lined with Art Deco buildings from the colonial era, and looking across the Huangpu River at the forest of skyscrapers on the Pudong side is one of the great urban views in the world. The river bends just enough to give you a shifting perspective as you walk. Colonial heritage on one side, modern ambition on the other, the water in between. The juxtaposition captures the entire arc of modern Chinese history in a single panorama.

Shanghai was where the French set up their concession, where most of the opium dens operated, where Nanjing Road became a symbol of foreign commercial dominance. Walking these streets after visiting the history museum underneath the Oriental Pearl Tower makes every block resonate differently. The French Concession today is all tree-lined boulevards, trendy coffee shops, and boutique stores. Hipster Shanghai. I didn't see that coming.

I explored it by bike, and even the cycling infrastructure was surprisingly good. Shanghai has more personality than its reputation for slick modernity suggests. It's not just rich and international. It's genuinely interesting to walk around, with layers of history that reward curiosity. For a city of this size and ambition, that's rarer than it should be.
